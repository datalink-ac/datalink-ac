title: Why it's called datalink.
date: 2026-11-17
order: 7
desc: The name we landed on, and the thinking that got us there.
tags: concept, naming
---

The name took a while to settle. We tried infolink, linkdata and dataurl, and
ended up with datalink.

(1) info

Information is not the thing. Every link carries information already. What we
meant was narrower: the numbers themselves, on the URL, with a service that
keeps nothing.

That is what lets a link be stateless. Tomorrow it still says the same thing,
and it says it even when the service behind it is down.

So: data rather than info.

(2) url

The people who will paste these links into a README or a post are not thinking
in URLs. They are thinking in links — and link is a word they already have.

dataurl had a second problem: it sounded like somewhere you go to download
data from. A repository of it, an endpoint, somewhere else. But the data is
not somewhere else. It is in the link you are already sending.

(3) linkdata

linkdata has the head in the wrong place. It reads as data attached to a link
— data is the subject, link is the modifier. We were after the other way
round: a kind of link, one that happens to carry the data.

(It is also a name that is already taken, which we found out late.)

(4) datalink

Data in the link.

That is the thing, and that is the name.
